# Example — LinkedIn review

This example is fictional.

## Input

Headline:

> Senior Product Designer | UX/UI | Design Systems | AI

About excerpt:

> I am a product designer with 12 years of experience creating digital products. I work with discovery, UX, UI, design systems, workshops, and AI tools. I enjoy solving complex problems and collaborating with multidisciplinary teams.

Recent experience excerpt:

> Led redesign initiatives for an enterprise platform. Created flows, prototypes, and interface specifications. Worked closely with Product and Engineering.

## Request

> Diagnose how this LinkedIn content currently positions the person. Do not rewrite.

## Looking Glass diagnosis

### Current read

**Inference:** The material reads as an experienced senior product designer with broad execution capability, but it does not yet make higher-scope decision ownership very visible.

### Strong signals

**Direct Evidence**
- 12 years of experience.
- Experience across discovery, UX, UI, design systems, workshops, and AI tools.
- Collaboration with Product and Engineering.
- Leadership of redesign initiatives.

### Narrative risks

**Inference**
- The headline lists domains and tools but does not create a clear professional point of view.
- "Led redesign initiatives" signals some leadership, but the visible evidence still emphasizes production artifacts such as flows, prototypes, and specifications.
- The breadth of topics may create a generalist read if the person is trying to signal a narrower staff-level role.

### Evidence gaps

The supplied material does not make these areas visible enough:

- size or complexity of the product context;
- what decisions the person owned;
- how their work affected other teams or systems;
- measurable or observable outcomes;
- whether leadership was project-level, product-level, or organizational.

This does not mean those experiences do not exist. They are simply not visible in the supplied material.

### Direction

Before rewriting, decide which professional signal should become primary:

1. broad senior product designer;
2. design systems specialist;
3. staff-level product designer focused on cross-functional product decisions.

The existing material can support the first direction directly. The third direction would require stronger evidence of scope and decision ownership.
