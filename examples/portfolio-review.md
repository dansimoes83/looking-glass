# Example — Portfolio review

This example is fictional.

## Input

Portfolio introduction:

> I design digital experiences that combine strategy, simplicity, and beautiful interfaces.

Case structure:

- hero image;
- problem statement;
- discovery process;
- wireframes;
- final screens;
- design system;
- final gallery.

## Request

> Use Looking Glass to diagnose what kind of designer this portfolio currently communicates.

## Looking Glass diagnosis

### Current read

**Inference:** The portfolio strongly signals visual and product-design craft, but the visible structure places more emphasis on process artifacts and final UI than on decisions, trade-offs, or influence.

### Strong signals

**Direct Evidence**
- Discovery and wireframing are part of the process.
- The work includes final interface design and design-system thinking.
- The introduction explicitly combines strategy and interface craft.

### Narrative risks

**Inference**
- "Strategy" is claimed, but the case structure does not yet show strategic decisions.
- The gallery-heavy ending may leave the strongest final impression around visual execution.
- The portfolio does not make personal ownership or cross-functional influence visible in the supplied structure.

### Evidence gap

The current material does not show enough about:

- major decisions;
- alternatives considered;
- trade-offs;
- personal responsibility;
- stakeholder influence;
- business or user outcomes.

### Direction

If the intended positioning is senior visual/product design, the current structure is broadly coherent.

If the intended positioning is staff-level product design or design leadership, the cases need stronger evidence of decision scope and influence before the introduction becomes more ambitious.
